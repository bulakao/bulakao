# Bulakao Business Notes

## Working Definition

Bulakao is a founder-led custom software studio for teams whose operations are outgrowing spreadsheets, chat threads, forms, and manual handoffs.

The first version of the business should be honest without underselling:

- Founder-led, not "big agency."
- Small and senior, not cheap freelance labor.
- AI-accelerated, not AI-hype-first.
- Forward-deployed in spirit: close to the user's workflow, fast to learn, fast to ship, accountable for the working system.
- Custom software when the workflow matters enough that generic tools, no-code builders, or another spreadsheet are no longer enough.

Plain-language positioning:

> Bulakao builds focused internal tools for teams stuck running important work through sheets, chat, and manual follow-ups.

Sharper version:

> Founder-led custom software for the internal workflows your team keeps patching manually.

## What Is True

Current operating reality:

- Bulakao starts as a one-person custom software agency.
- The work should be designed around founder capacity until the business can support collaborators, contractors, or full-time hires.
- The founder should stay close to discovery, product judgment, architecture, and delivery quality.
- AI should be used heavily for research, prototyping, coding acceleration, testing support, documentation, and maintenance, but final responsibility stays human.
- The tech stack should stay pragmatic. The default stack is likely Next.js, Supabase, Vercel/GitHub Pages where appropriate, and whatever integrations the client workflow requires.

What should not be said publicly:

- "One-man agency."
- "I can build anything."
- "AI will automate your business."
- "Cheap apps fast."
- "We replace your team with agents."

What can be said publicly:

- "Founder-led."
- "Direct access to the builder."
- "Built close to the workflow."
- "Small, focused builds."
- "Useful first version, then improve from real use."
- "AI-assisted where it helps; software that still works when judgment matters."

## Why This Business Exists

Many growing teams do not need a full digital transformation. They need one painful workflow made usable.

Common symptoms:

- A spreadsheet has become a system of record.
- Work starts in chat, then gets copied into a sheet, then followed up manually.
- Managers ask for the same status updates every week.
- Client updates require hunting through email, Slack, documents, and project tools.
- Inventory, approvals, reports, or handoffs depend on one person's memory.
- The business already tried generic SaaS, but the tool did not match the actual workflow.

Bulakao should sell the removal of operational drag, not "software development" as an abstract service.

## Market Read

Research signal as of July 2026:

- AI adoption is high, but deep workflow integration is still uneven. McKinsey's 2025 survey says 88% of respondents report regular AI use in at least one business function, but only one-third are scaling AI across their organizations; smaller companies lag further behind.
- AI agents are becoming a boardroom topic, but the market is noisy. Gartner predicts 40% of enterprise applications will include task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, while also warning that over 40% of agentic AI projects may be canceled by the end of 2027 because of cost, unclear value, or weak risk controls.
- The best opportunity is not to sell "agents." It is to sell clear workflows, useful internal tools, and carefully applied automation.
- Forrester frames agentic software development as agents doing meaningful SDLC work while humans remain accountable for intent, review, and outcomes. That maps well to Bulakao's internal operating model: use AI to increase leverage, but keep founder judgment visible in delivery.
- The forward-deployed model is increasingly relevant. Palantir describes forward deployed software engineers as embedding with customers and focusing on many capabilities for one customer. SVPG describes the model as empowered engineers spending intense time with customers to learn the problem and discover the solution that achieves the outcome.
- Workflow and internal-tool platforms validate demand. Airtable, AppSheet, and Retool all market around the same pain: teams need workflows, apps, dashboards, integrations, and automation around real business processes. Bulakao's opportunity is not to out-platform them; it is to provide judgment, implementation, and ownership for teams that do not want to figure it out alone.
- Business process automation is a growing category. Fortune Business Insights estimates the global business process automation market at $19.85B in 2025, projected to $56.68B by 2034.

Implication:

Bulakao should not compete as "another AI agency." The stronger lane is:

> A founder-led, AI-accelerated internal software studio that turns one messy workflow into a working tool.

## Philippines Target Thesis

Yes, Bulakao can target the Philippines.

The Philippines is a strong starting market because the pain is real: many businesses are operating across chat, spreadsheets, manual approvals, finance admin, client updates, inventory trackers, and repeated status reporting. The market also has enough English fluency, services culture, and operational complexity to support a founder-led software studio.

But the target should not be "Philippine MSMEs" broadly. That is too wide, too price-sensitive, and too vague.

Better starting target:

> Philippine SMBs and service teams with 10-200 people whose operations are already too complex for spreadsheets and chat.

Even sharper:

> Philippine service, operations, and back-office teams that need one internal workflow turned into reliable software.

Why the Philippines makes sense:

- The business base is large. PSA 2023 establishment data lists about 1.1 million micro establishments, 109,910 small establishments, and 4,763 medium establishments nationwide.
- MSMEs employ a large share of the workforce. The World Bank notes that MSMEs employ over 60% of the Philippine workforce.
- Digitalization is a national priority, but adoption still has gaps. The World Bank says digital transformation has been a Philippine priority since 2022, but progress has been slow because broadband, policy, skills, and adoption remain uneven.
- This creates a middle opportunity: not huge enterprise transformation, and not cheap one-off automation. Bulakao can help capable teams adopt practical internal software one workflow at a time.

The market risk:

- Many microbusinesses cannot afford custom software.
- Some owners will compare custom software against low-cost freelancers, no-code tools, or one-time automation gigs.
- Connectivity, digital readiness, and process maturity vary a lot by region and industry.
- Some businesses will want a website, POS setup, social media automation, or generic IT support. That is not the core offer.

The commercial implication:

Bulakao should target Philippine businesses, but qualify for:

- At least 10 employees, ideally 20-200.
- A recurring workflow, not a one-time task.
- A clear owner or manager who feels the pain.
- Existing revenue and willingness to pay for operational improvement.
- A process already being run through sheets, chat, email, forms, PDFs, or a mismatched SaaS tool.
- A first build that can be scoped without months of enterprise integration.

## Positioning Principles

### Lead With The Workflow

The buyer does not wake up wanting Next.js, Supabase, agents, or custom software architecture.

The buyer feels:

- "This process is too manual."
- "I do not trust this spreadsheet anymore."
- "This report takes too long."
- "Approvals keep getting lost."
- "Clients keep asking for updates."
- "The team is working around the tools instead of through them."

The site and sales call should start there.

### Use Founder-Led As A Strength

Do not hide that Bulakao is founder-led. Do not make it sound like a large team either.

The premium version of the truth:

- The person scoping the work is close to the person building it.
- Less account-manager translation.
- Faster decisions.
- Fewer handoffs.
- Limited capacity by design.
- Senior attention on a narrow problem.

Good language:

- "Founder-led builds."
- "Work directly with the builder."
- "A small studio for focused internal tools."
- "Limited active projects so the build stays close to the workflow."

Avoid:

- "Solo developer."
- "Freelancer."
- "One-person agency."
- "Boutique" unless it feels natural later.

### Make AI A Delivery Advantage, Not The Product

AI should be part of the operating model:

- Faster prototypes.
- Faster code generation and refactoring.
- Better test coverage and documentation support.
- Faster research into client tools and APIs.
- Optional AI features when the workflow benefits from judgment, summarization, routing, extraction, or classification.

But the promise should stay grounded:

> Software your team can use, maintain, and trust.

Risks:

### Hypothesis 3: Inventory, order, and fulfillment teams

Examples:

- E-commerce operators.
- Import/export teams.

Pain:

- Dashboards and workflows have obvious before/after value.

Examples:

- Expense tracking.

Risks:

For the first 3-5 clients, stay Philippines-first, horizontal by workflow, and narrow by problem type:

> Internal tools for approvals, reporting, client updates, inventory, and operational handoffs.

This is narrow enough to sell and build repeatably, but not so narrow that Bulakao gets stuck before learning from the market.

The site can say:

> Send the handoff, report, approval, or tracker your team keeps patching manually.

Then use sales conversations to learn which niche has the strongest pull.

Priority early outreach:

1. Philippine service businesses with 10-200 people.
2. Philippine agencies, BPO-adjacent teams, and professional services firms.
3. Philippine inventory/order/admin-heavy operators.

Do not start with the smallest microbusinesses unless the owner has unusually strong pain, budget, and speed.

Goal:

Output:

- Bottleneck diagnosis.
- First-build recommendation.
- Rough scope and next step.

Positioning:

- "Send the rough version. If there is not a clear tool to build, we will tell you."

### 2. First Useful Version

Goal:

- Build the smallest tool that removes real drag.

Likely scope:

- Internal dashboard.
- Approval queue.
- Client update portal.
- Inventory tracker.
- Reporting view.
- Intake and routing workflow.
- Admin tool.

Delivery:

- 2-4 week target for small scoped tools, adjusted based on integrations and data readiness.

Definition of done:

- Real users can complete the core workflow.
- Basic permissions are handled.
- Data is stored in a sane place.
- The team has a simple operating handoff.
- Known limitations are documented.

### 3. Monthly Improvement

Goal:

- Improve the tool after real use.
- Add integrations, automation, AI support, reports, permissions, or new workflows.

Why it matters:

- Most custom software fails when the builder disappears after v1.
- Bulakao should sell continuity without trapping clients in vague retainers.

Possible structure:

- Fixed monthly capacity.
- Priority backlog.
- Maintenance and small improvements.
- Larger features scoped separately.

## Delivery Model

### Default Stack

Use the simplest stack that can responsibly support the workflow.

Likely default:

- Next.js for app UI and server logic.
- Supabase for Postgres, auth, storage, and realtime where useful.
- Vercel for deployment when appropriate.
- GitHub for version control.
- Resend/Postmark for transactional email if needed.
- Stripe if billing or payments are needed.
- APIs and webhooks for client tools.
- LLM APIs or agent workflows only where they create clear workflow value.

Alternatives:

- Retool or similar internal-tool platforms when speed and governance matter more than custom UI.
- Airtable/AppSheet/Zapier/Make/n8n when no-code or workflow automation is enough.
- Custom API/backend when integrations, permissions, data model, or product complexity require it.

Rule:

Bulakao should recommend the right level of software, not force custom code when a simpler tool solves the problem.

### AI Operating Model

Use AI internally for leverage:

- Discovery synthesis.
- Competitive and API research.
- Architecture drafts.
- Code generation.
- Test generation.
- Refactoring.
- Documentation.
- QA checklists.
- Support note summaries.

Guardrails:

- Human review before shipping.
- Tests or manual QA for critical flows.
- No blind AI-generated security decisions.
- No sensitive client data in tools without permission and appropriate controls.
- Keep architecture and business rules understandable by humans.

### Competitor And Market Pricing Read

Research signal as of July 2026:

- Symph's The Agency is the closest positioning competitor. It sells custom software around small business workflows through a monthly subscription. Public pricing starts at $1,500/month for US visitors, with localized Philippine site code showing ₱85,000/month. Its value proposition is no large upfront custom build fee, first working version, monthly improvements, and same team/same context.
- Symph's main company site is more enterprise-grade: AI software development, custom AI apps, web platforms, mobile apps, and enterprise systems backed by 16 years of engineering. That is above Bulakao's current weight class, but it validates the AI-accelerated custom software lane.
- Socia is a direct local positioning competitor around custom business systems and automation. Its public copy says "Custom software and automation that cut manual work, speed up sales, and scale as you grow." Its public intake flow includes budget bands under ₱100,000, ₱100,000-₱300,000, ₱300,000-₱500,000, and ₱500,000+, and its FAQ says most projects start in the ₱100,000 range. Simple systems can launch in 4-6 weeks; larger platforms take a few months.
- Philippine custom app estimates from Manila Recruitment put small/basic apps at PHP150,000-PHP400,000, medium apps at PHP400,000-PHP1.5M, and large complex apps at PHP1.5M+.
- Philippine software development agencies and outsourcing firms commonly quote hourly or blended rates. Mashup Garage cites Philippine software development rates around $25-$60/hour. Hireplicity's 2026 guide puts mid-level agency/BPO rates around $25-$40/hour and senior agency/BPO rates around $40-$55/hour.
- Direct-hire Filipino developers can be cheaper than agencies, but that is not the right comparison for Bulakao. Bulakao is selling scoping, product judgment, architecture, implementation, and ongoing ownership, not just developer hours.

Pricing implication:

Bulakao should not compete below freelancers. It should sit between low-cost freelancers/no-code implementers and larger teams like Symph, Socia, Mashup Garage, White Cloak, and similar custom software shops.

The credible early-market range:

- Under ₱100,000: avoid custom software unless it is a paid diagnostic, prototype, no-code automation, or extremely narrow internal tool.
- ₱100,000-₱300,000: good first-build range for a focused internal tool with limited integrations.
- ₱300,000-₱500,000: good range for multi-role workflows, dashboards, client portals, reporting, and light integrations.
- ₱500,000+: should involve bigger scope, heavier integrations, compliance, mobile apps, or contractors/collaborators.
- ₱85,000/month: credible subscription anchor because Symph has already publicized this locally. Bulakao can use this later as a build subscription or improvement plan, but should not publish it until the offer is tested.

#### 3. First Useful Build

Price:

- ₱150,000-₱350,000 fixed scope.

Use when:

- One workflow.
- One core user group or a small number of roles.
- Basic auth and permissions.
- Simple dashboard or admin interface.
- One main data model.
- Limited integrations.
- 2-6 week target.

Examples:

- Approval tracker.
- Client update portal.
- Internal reporting dashboard.
- Inventory/order board.
- Intake and follow-up tracker.

Payment:

- 50% upfront.
- 30% on working beta.

Alternative:

- ₱85,000/month, 2-month minimum, if the client prefers subscription and the scope will keep evolving.

#### 4. Larger Operations System

Price:

- ₱350,000-₱750,000+.

Use when:

- Multiple roles.
- More complex data models.
- Client-facing portal.
- Reporting and exports.
- Integrations with existing tools.
- Audit trail, files, notifications, or automation.
- 6-12 week target.

For this tier, consider bringing in trusted contractors for design, QA, data migration, or specific integrations.

#### 5. Monthly Improvement And Care

Price:

- Care: ₱25,000-₱45,000/month.
- Improve: ₱60,000-₱95,000/month.
- Priority build: ₱120,000-₱180,000/month.

Use when:

- The first tool is live.
- The client wants continuous improvements.
- There are ongoing reports, fixes, integrations, and workflow changes.

Scope examples:

- Care: monitoring, bug fixes, small copy/config changes, dependency updates, light support.
- Improve: small features, report changes, workflow refinements, automations, training notes.
- Priority build: active monthly feature development with reserved founder capacity.

Charge monthly upfront. Third-party services, API fees, SMS, email volume, hosting, storage, and paid AI usage should be billed separately or clearly included with limits.

### Pricing Rules

- Do not price by "AI made me faster." Price by outcome, risk, scope, and ownership.
- Do not take custom-code projects below ₱100,000 unless they are intentionally tiny, strategic, or part of a paid learning phase.
- Do not publish cheap starter packages. They attract the wrong buyer.
- Use "starts at" language only when ready to handle inbound qualification.
- Separate build cost from ongoing software costs.
- Add a maintenance path to every build. Unmaintained custom software creates future pain.
- Protect founder capacity. A low-priced client can still consume high-priced attention.
- Increase price for urgency, messy data, compliance, payment flows, integrations, and client-facing reliability.

## Niche Selection Scorecard

Score each candidate from 1-5:

- Pain intensity: Is the workflow painful enough to fund?
- Urgency: Does the buyer need it soon?
- Budget: Can they pay for custom software?
- Access: Can we reach them through network, content, or outbound?
- Repeatability: Will projects share patterns?
- Integration complexity: Can v1 be built without months of enterprise integration?
- Risk: Are compliance, downtime, or data sensitivity manageable?
- Founder interest: Is this a market worth learning deeply?

Good early niches score high on pain, urgency, access, repeatability, and founder interest, while keeping risk manageable.
